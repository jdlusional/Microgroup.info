Live Products

Funder & grant match

Almanac

Finds the funders and open grants that fit an organization, ranks them, and shows the public source behind every one. It works from that organization's own public record and builds the list against its actual goals and programs rather than a keyword match on its mission statement. Scribe, Docket, and Confluence are add-ons built on it, and each requires an active Almanac subscription.

Grant drafter

Scribe

Works from an Almanac match to produce a near-submission draft of the grant application, fact-checked against the organization's own public information. It returns the draft with an explicit list of the facts it could not verify, which only the applicant can supply. Facts about the funder are quoted from its own page or marked unconfirmed. Requires an active Almanac subscription.

Organization Project Requests

Docket

Almanac's sibling for Organization Project Requests, meaning Letters of Inquiry and Requests for Proposals, tracked separately from traditional grants. Same sourcing standard: every request traces to the funder's or agency's own current page, and the amounts, windows, and eligibility rules are quoted from it or marked unconfirmed. Requires an active Almanac subscription.

Joint funding, two or more organizations

Confluence

Finds funding that connects your organization's mission to one or more named partner organizations' work, including consortiums, associations, and collaboratives, where you remain the eligible applicant. An angle Almanac's single-org matching doesn't surface on its own. Every organization in the group needs its own Almanac subscription; only one needs Scribe, and only one Docket.

Why these rather than a grant database

A subscription database is curated before the vendor knows a specific organization exists. Someone decides which opportunities belong on the shelf, and every subscriber sees the same shelf.

These products reverse that order: a person who has worked in the nonprofit sector reads the organization first, and its shelf is built afterward, against its actual goals and programs rather than a keyword match on its mission statement. That is a claim an organization can test before it pays anything, because every demo above is shown before price is discussed.

Why these rather than a general research tool

The funder records behind these products sit on one EIN spine alongside sources a grants database does not carry:

Joining those to the grant record is what makes a single question answerable in one pass: which funders support this work, are growing their giving, and carry no adverse audit findings.
