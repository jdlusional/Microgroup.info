Docket

Docket carries Organization Project Requests, meaning Letters of Inquiry and Requests for Proposals, separately from Almanac's traditional grant matches. This page runs Docket against a made-up organization so you can see the output before you commission your own.

Method

How this was built

TopicWhat it means
Where this comes from Every funder and every request on this page is drawn from that funder's own public record: its IRS Form 990 filings, its published guidelines, and its own application page.

Each card carries a link back to the source it came from, so nothing here has to be taken on trust.

Where a funder does not publish a figure (an award range, a deadline), that gap is marked as unconfirmed rather than filled in.
What Docket will not do It does not invent a fact about a funder.

An unpublished deadline is reported as unpublished, not replaced with a plausible-looking date.

A closed or paused process is recorded as closed, not left showing as open because it once was. Two of the six candidates researched for this page were exactly that case, caught by re-checking each funder's own current page rather than trusting an earlier pass.
What it cannot tell you An open Letter of Inquiry route is not a promise of funding: an LOI is a first step, not an award, and most of what looks open on a funder's page is genuinely a long-odds door rather than a sure thing.

Funder priorities and application windows move, sometimes without notice; every row on this page was checked against the funder's own current page on 2026-08-11, a snapshot rather than a promise.

Confirm the current cycle, deadline, and eligibility on the funder's own site before applying.
Why this and not an aggregator listing Letters of Inquiry and Requests for Proposals are the part of the funding landscape that goes stale fastest, because they open and close on the funder's own schedule and often without an announcement anywhere else. An aggregator restates what it last saw. Docket reads the funder's or agency's own current page and records what is there on the date it checked, which is why every card above carries that date.

This is measurable on this page rather than asserted. Of the seventeen candidates checked to build it, two were open on an earlier pass and were not open when re-checked directly, one paused to new applicants and one closed outright. An aggregator listing would have shown both as live.

The second difference is what happens to a negative result. Every determination made here, including every closed, not-yet-open and ruled-out one, is kept permanently in the underlying opportunity register whether or not it ever appears on a client's page. A catalog product has no reason to record that something was checked and found closed; here it is the record that stops the same answer being paid for twice.

What this does not claim: Docket does not find more LOI and RFP calls than exist. These calls are genuinely rarer than traditional grants, and a refresh that confirms there is nothing new is a real result rather than a failed one.
How candidates are found and checked Candidates are found by searching for funders whose own published guidelines or program pages name community development, food security, youth and family stability, or immigrant/refugee support in a geography that plausibly includes this applicant, then checking each one directly against the funder's own current page rather than an aggregator (Instrumentl, Candid, GrantForward and similar restate and go stale; several determinations on this page contradicted what an aggregator would have shown).

Seventeen candidates were checked to build this page: five are genuinely open today and shown above, two were ruled out on direct verification after previously being open (one paused to new applicants since an earlier check, one closed outright), and ten more turned out closed, not-yet-open, or a genuine mismatch on first contact. Most annual-cycle funders checked in this pass had already closed for the year by the August check date; the five shown above are open specifically because they run on a rolling basis or fall inside a live seasonal window, not an annual one.

Every determination, including every closed and ruled-out one, is captured permanently in the underlying opportunity register regardless of whether it ends up shown on any specific client's page, so the next search does not have to pay to find the same answer twice.
