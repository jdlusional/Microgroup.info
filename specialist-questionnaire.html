Your Professional Profile

You agreed to join MICRO Group's specialist panel. MICRO Group calls this role a specialist; the same role is also commonly called a consultant, and the two terms are used interchangeably on this form and in your agreement with the firm. This form collects the professional details the firm needs in order to describe you accurately, and it asks separately, field by field, what you are willing to have published.

Why this exists. When MICRO Group responds to a request for proposals, it is often required to name the people who would do the work and attach their credentials. Describing you from public sources alone produces errors. This form gets the facts from you instead.

Nothing here is a commitment. Completing it does not sign you to anything, does not oblige you to accept any work, and does not entitle you to any. Those terms live in a separate memorandum you will see before anything is signed.

Publication is separate from disclosure. Telling us something is not permission to publish it. The consent section at the end asks about each field on its own, and anything you leave blank there is treated as a no.

Most questions are required. If one genuinely does not apply, write "None" or "N/A" rather than leaving it blank, so we can tell the difference between "nothing to report" and "not answered yet."

Not part of your answers: what public sources already say about you

Before writing to you we looked you up in public sources, because a panel roster with a wrong credential on it is worse than no roster at all.
