Our Story Since 2000

We'd rather fix your bike right than make you feel dumb for asking.

Clown Dog Bikes has been on San Jacinto Blvd, just north of the UT Austin campus, since 2000. Twenty-five years in, the shop is still built around one idea: treat every rider — first flat tire or fiftieth century ride — like they belong here.

How it started

John Chisholm and Bryce Holt opened Clown Dog Bikes in 2000, a block from campus, with a simple bet: a bike shop could be genuinely welcoming and still know exactly what it's doing. A 2025 profile in Community Impact marked the shop's 25th year in business, still in the same stretch of San Jacinto Blvd where it started.

That location was never an accident. Being the closest full-service shop to UT Austin meant the daily customer was as likely to be a freshman who'd never touched a derailleur as it was a longtime commuter. The shop was built to work for both.

The anti-elitist streak

Bike shops have a reputation, earned in plenty of places, for being cliquish — the kind of counter where an amateur question gets an eye-roll. Chisholm and Holt built Clown Dog Bikes as the opposite of that on purpose. In a profile with The Austinot, the founders described pushing back directly against that culture: no gatekeeping, no attitude, no assumption that you should already know the answer.

That shows up in concrete ways. The shop has offered DIY repair videos so customers can learn to handle simple fixes themselves instead of paying for every small job. And when Clown Dog genuinely isn't the right source for something — the Austinot piece cites an example of tracking down a hard-to-find 24-inch wheelchair wheel from elsewhere — the answer is a referral, not a stall.

What the shop actually is, day to day

Repair is the core of the business, not a side counter to the retail floor. A 2025 press profile put the shop at roughly 500 repairs a month — that volume is the real center of gravity here, more than any single bike sale.

The reviews track the same story. Customers consistently point to staff going out of their way to help, patience with beginners and out-of-practice riders, pricing that comes in fair — sometimes under the original estimate — and turnaround that doesn't drag. None of it reads as a sales pitch; it reads as a shop that just does the work.
