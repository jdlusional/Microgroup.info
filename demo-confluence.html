Confluence

Confluence finds funding that connects your organization's mission to one or more named partner organizations' work, including consortiums, associations, and collaboratives, where you remain the eligible applicant. It is the angle Almanac's single-org matching does not surface on its own. This page runs Confluence against two made-up organizations so you can see the output before you commission your own, one instance of a bridge Confluence supports for two or more organizations.

How to read this page

TopicWhat it means
Every organization involved Sprout Circle Early Learning and Ascend Workforce Partners do not exist.

They are illustrative East Austin community organizations invented for this demo, and every figure attached to either was invented along with it.

Naming a real organization as the fictional applicant's partner would assert a relationship involving a real entity that doesn't exist, so every side of the pairing is fictional, not just one. This demo illustrates the simplest case, a single named partner, but Confluence works the same way for two or more partners: a small consortium, a member association, or a larger collaborative.
Who applies Sprout Circle Early Learning is the eligible applicant on every candidate below. Ascend Workforce Partners is named as a formal service partner in the narrative, not a co-applicant on any submission. This mirrors how Confluence works for a real client, whether there is one named partner or several: your organization applies, one or more named partners' missions are the bridge, not a joint application with multiple signatories.
What each organization needs Every organization in the group needs its own active Almanac subscription, not the lead applicant only: Confluence's matches are only as good as the profile research already done for each member, and that research is Almanac's job.

Only one organization in the group needs an active Scribe subscription, to draft joint applications on the group's behalf regardless of which member's funder match a candidate came from.

Only one organization in the group needs an active Docket subscription, to source and track any Letter of Inquiry or Request for Proposal opportunity the group finds.
The funders and requests The funders are real.

Each one is an actual foundation with a live, currently open application route, and each carries a link to its own page.

Deadlines, eligibility, and program scope are quoted from the funder's own page, or marked as unconfirmed where the funder does not publish them.

What is illustrative is the match itself: the judgment that this funder's own stated mission genuinely spans every organization's work involved, not a single one.
Tiers, not a flat list Candidates are sorted Pursue, Consider, and Checked & Ruled Out, the same three-tier structure used on the real, currently deployed Confluence add-on. A funder in Consider has a real but weaker or more conditional connection to the full set of organizations involved than one in Pursue; a funder in Checked & Ruled Out was found and verified, then excluded for a stated reason, shown rather than dropped silently.

Method

How this was built

TopicWhat it means
How this differs from Almanac and Docket Almanac ranks funders and open grants against a single organization's own profile. Docket does the same for Letters of Inquiry and Requests for Proposals. Confluence starts from a group of two or more: it looks for the intersection between the applicant's eligibility and one or more named partners' missions, whether that partner is a single organization, a consortium, a member association, or a larger collaborative, then checks whether a real funder's own stated priorities genuinely span every organization involved. It is a small, hand-curated shortlist rather than a ranked, recurring cycle: revisit it if any organization's work, or the partnership itself, changes.
How this is purchased Confluence is priced as a one-time engagement, not an annual subscription, because that is what it actually is: a dedicated cross-organization research pass built once, then optionally refreshed on a standing cadence. Full rates: See Pricing →
Where this comes from Every funder on this page is drawn from that funder's own public record: its IRS Form 990 filings where relevant, its published guidelines, and its own application page.

Each card carries a link back to the source it came from, so nothing here has to be taken on trust.

Where a funder does not publish a figure (an award range, a deadline), that gap is marked as unconfirmed rather than filled in.
What Confluence will not do It does not stretch a funder's stated mission to manufacture a connection that isn't genuinely there; a weak or conditional fit is placed in Consider, not Pursue, and a candidate with no honest bridging angle at all is placed in Checked & Ruled Out rather than forced into either live tier.

It does not name a real organization as the fictional applicant's partner, whether that partner is a single organization or a larger consortium, association, or collaborative. Every organization on this page is invented for exactly that reason.
How candidates are found and checked Candidates are found by searching for funders whose own published guidelines name both halves of the intersection this pairing sits on (early childhood/family support and family economic security, in this case), preferring funders that accept unsolicited Letters of Inquiry on a rolling basis over those tied to an annual cycle, since most annual-cycle funders checked in this pass had already closed for the year by the August check date.

Seven candidates were checked to build this page: five carry a genuine live connection and are shown in Pursue or Consider, and two were ruled out on direct verification, one because its Letter of Inquiry process is currently paused during a foundation leadership transition, one because its geography and its next open cycle both fall outside this pairing's reach.

Every determination, including the two ruled-out ones, is captured permanently in the underlying opportunity register regardless of whether it ends up shown on any specific client's page.
