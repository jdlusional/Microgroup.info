What this is, and why it exists

TopicWhat it means
Where this came from On August 11, 2026, Meme Styles (Measure's President) shared that three projects in Measure's pipeline — Huston-Tillotson University, the City of Austin Homelessness Strategy Office, and Texas Empowerment Academy (TEA) — had all been postponed for funding shortages. TEA specifically lost approximately $300,000 in anticipated funding. Rather than let the relationship and prior work go to waste, Meme asked directly: could Almanac identify funding where Measure and TEA could collaborate on a joint project, mutually beneficial to both — naming K-12 education research specifically. Confluence is the direct answer to that question, built the same day.
What this is A small, hand-verified shortlist of real, currently open (or recently closed, marked as such) funding opportunities where the funder's own stated mission connects to both Measure's evaluation/data-justice work and TEA's culturally relevant K-12 education work in East Austin — and where Measure itself, not TEA, is the eligible applicant. Every candidate below was checked directly against the funder's own current page, not an aggregator, the same sourcing standard as the rest of this page.
How it differs from Almanac above Almanac ranks funders and open grants against Measure's own profile alone. Confluence specifically looks for the intersection between Measure's eligibility and a named partner organization's mission — an angle Almanac's single-org matching doesn't surface on its own. It is a one-time curated add-on, not a recurring cycle: revisit it if the TEA collaboration's funding picture changes, rather than expecting it to refresh automatically.
Method Built from a corpus of 704 funder-eligibility determinations already hand-verified specifically for Measure and sitting in the underlying pipeline data, filtered for candidates thematically adjacent to TEA's mission, then independently re-checked against each funder's own current page for this pass. Full write-up, including two candidates dropped for stale information and one genuine data discrepancy flagged rather than resolved: see the source note at the bottom of this section.
