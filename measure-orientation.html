What Almanac and Scribe are

Two tools, one shelf. Almanac decides what you are eligible for. Scribe writes against what Almanac decided, and never decides anything itself.

Almanac

Almanac is the shelf. It joins your IRS Form 990 record against a hand-curated register of funders and open calls, ranks what fits, and records what does not fit and why. Every eligibility question on this page was answered by Almanac. Nothing on this page re-derives it.

That single-source rule is the whole design. An earlier build let a second component re-check eligibility for itself, and three parts of the same system reported three different totals for this organization on the same day. Opportunities the shelf had already excluded reached draft stage anyway. The shelf now decides once and everything downstream reads the answer.

Scribe

Scribe is the drafter. It assembles an application draft from your profile, your 990 figures and the funder’s own stated priorities, and it marks every place where it does not have a fact rather than inventing one. A Scribe draft is a starting document with the gaps labelled, not a finished submission.

          Appendix · technical Method

          Method, provenance and stated limits

          Written for a reader who wants to audit the numbers rather than use them. Nothing here is required to read the seven sections above.

          Almanac: where this comes from

          Every funder and every grant on your shelf is drawn from that funder's own public record: its IRS Form 990 filings, its published guidelines, and its own grant pages.

          Each match carries a link back to the source it came from, so nothing has to be taken on trust.

          Where a funder does not publish a figure, an award range, a deadline, an eligibility rule, that gap is marked as unconfirmed rather than filled in.

          Almanac: what it will not do

          It does not invent a fact about a funder.

          An unpublished deadline is reported as unpublished, not replaced with a plausible-looking date, because a made-up deadline is worse than a missing one: a missing deadline gets checked, and an invented one gets relied on.

          It also keeps a hard line between a funder that has actually given to this organization before and a funder that merely looks like a good fit. A candidate is never dressed up as a confirmed prior funder.

          Almanac: what it cannot tell you

          Form 990 filings reach the public a year or more after the fact, so a funder's priorities may already have moved.

          A foundation that funds this kind of work is not therefore open to you: many accept no unsolicited proposals at all, and some of the best matches here will be closed doors for reasons no filing records.

          The ranking is a judgment about fit, not a prediction of success. Treat every row as a lead to verify against the funder's own page before you spend real time on it.

          Scribe: where this comes from

          Each draft is built from two sources and no others: the Almanac match that identified the funder, with that funder's own published requirements, and the organization's own public record, its website, its filings, and the materials it has already put into the world.

          Every substantive claim in a draft traces back to one of those two places.

          Scribe: what it will not do

          It does not invent facts about the organization.

          When an application asks for something only the applicant holds, a budget line, an outcome figure, a board resolution, a letter of support, Scribe does not guess at it. It leaves the space empty and puts the item on the list of what is still needed.

          This is the whole discipline of the tool: an invented number is worse than a blank one, because a blank gets filled in before it is filed and an invention gets submitted.

          Scribe: what it cannot tell you

          A draft is a draft, not a submission.

          Funder requirements change, portals impose their own formats and character limits, and the published guidance a draft was built from can already be out of date.

          Everything here has to be checked against the funder's live application before it is filed, and the organization's own people are the only ones who can confirm that what it says about them is true today.

          Scribe is built to remove the blank page. It is not built to remove the review.
